2. METHOD, PRECISELY STATED  [NEW SECTION]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
It is important to distinguish two things that both get called "fragment-based" in the
computational spectroscopy literature, because they support different strength of claim:

  (a) TRUE FRAGMENT QUANTUM CHEMISTRY (not what this project does): methods such as the
      Molecular Tailoring Approach (MTA), Molecules-in-Molecules (MIM), ONIOM, and DFTB
      fragment the QUANTUM CALCULATION itself -- they cut a large molecule into
      overlapping pieces, compute Hessians/tensors per piece, and stitch them back into
      ONE vibrational spectrum for the intact large structure, with boundary corrections
      so adjacent fragments "feel" each other (covalent linkage, H-bonding, conformational
      context). This is the literature's standard answer to "the molecule is too big to
      simulate whole."

  (b) SPECTRAL MIXTURE ANALYSIS / CLASSICAL LEAST SQUARES (what this project does): five
      INDEPENDENT DFT calculations on isolated free monomers (KDO, D-glucosamine,
      L-glycero-D-manno-heptose, myristic acid, phosphoric acid), each broadened and
      normalized on its own, then linearly recombined AFTER THE FACT via non-negative
      least squares (NNLS) against the experimental spectrum. This is a well-established,
      separately-published technique -- it is the same family as Multivariate Curve
      Resolution (MCR-ALS), routinely used to decompose complex biological Raman spectra
      into reference components with non-negativity constraints.

This project uses method (b). That is a legitimate, defensible, and commonly used
technique for exactly this scenario (identifying/quantifying known reference compounds
within a complex biomolecular Raman spectrum). It is NOT, however, a simulation of the
intact LPS molecule or its assembled core -- the five fragments are modeled as fully
independent, non-interacting species, and the fit result should be described and
interpreted accordingly (see Section 7).

INTERPRETATION: two of the four literature diagnostic peaks (carbohydrate/KDO at ~989,
lipid chain at ~1131) are reproduced within a tight tolerance in the experimental

inter-fragment coupling" limitations already noted in Section 7, and are worth flagging
explicitly in the thesis discussion rather than only reporting the two peaks that agree.

7. WHY IS R^2 ONLY 0.18? A QUANTITATIVE SENSITIVITY CHECK  [NEW SECTION]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
QUESTION: is the low R^2 caused by DFT lineshape/intensity inaccuracy (fixable by
allowing the reference spectra some flexibility), or by something structurally missing
from the model (non-interacting fragments, no inter-unit coupling, incomplete fragment
set)?

METHOD: a regularized, single-spectrum "soft alternating fit" (MCR-ALS-inspired) was
implemented. Because there is only ONE experimental spectrum, canonical multi-sample
MCR-ALS (which resolves concentrations and pure-component spectra jointly across many
mixture samples) is not well-posed here -- this is stated explicitly so the method is not
over-claimed. Instead, each fragment's spectral SHAPE is allowed to deviate from its DFT
reference by an amount controlled by a Tikhonov regularization strength (expressed as a
multiplier k; larger k = more tightly anchored to the DFT reference, smaller k = more
freedom), alternated with an NNLS re-fit of the weights. The maximum and median relative
deviation of the adjusted shapes from the DFT reference is tracked at every k, so the
amount of "cheating" being done at each fit-quality level is explicit and auditable.

RESULT (see output/tables/alternative_fit_comparison.xlsx, sheet "lambda_sweep", and
output/plots/regularized_fit_vs_nnls.png for the full sweep and figure):

  - Constraining the reference shapes to deviate by no more than ~20-25% from the DFT
    prediction (roughly the scale of typical B3LYP/6-31G(d) intensity/lineshape
    uncertainty) buys only a small, incremental improvement in fit quality over the
    plain NNLS fit.
  - Fit quality only improves substantially (large jumps in R^2) once the fragments are
    allowed to deviate WELL beyond plausible DFT accuracy (100%+ relative shape change).

CONCLUSION: the residual mismatch is not primarily explained by DFT lineshape/intensity
inaccuracy within its normal error range. It is more consistent with genuinely missing
physics: the non-interacting-fragment assumption (no covalent/H-bond coupling between
KDO, heptose, glucosamine, the acyl chain, and phosphate in real LPS), the incomplete
fragment set (outer-core sugars not modeled), and the use of a single experimental
replicate. This is a stronger, more defensible thesis claim than reporting R^2 alone,
because it rules out the "the DFT spectra are just a bit off" explanation quantitatively
rather than by assertion.

CAVEAT FOR THESIS USE: this sensitivity analysis was run in a separate reproduction
environment without direct access to the exact original scipy-based ALS/interpolation
pipeline that produced the official RMSE=0.185/R^2=0.180 numbers above; the reproduced
baseline in that environment showed the same fragment weights and the identical Pearson
r (0.5078) as the official result -- confirming the fit itself reproduces correctly --
but a slightly different absolute RMSE/R^2 due to a normalization-window difference (see
Section 8). The QUALITATIVE conclusion above (large deviation required before fit quality
improves materially) is robust to this offset, since it is about the shape of the
sensitivity curve, not its absolute starting point. For a thesis-exact sweep table,
re-run mcr_peak_validation.py's regularization sweep logic inside the original working
environment that produced model_metrics.xlsx.

8. REPRODUCIBILITY NOTE  [NEW SECTION -- IMPORTANT]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
While preparing Sections 6-7, an inconsistency was found between the run_final.py script
currently in the project folder and the output it is supposed to have produced:

  - run_final.py currently sets RAMAN_MIN=400, RAMAN_MAX=3200 cm-1, but this report and
    model_metrics.xlsx were generated on a 200-2000 cm-1 window (1801 grid points) --
    i.e. the script on disk does not match the parameters used to produce the saved
    results.
  - Separately, the experimental spectrum's min-max normalization in the current script
    is computed over the FULL loaded spectrum (native range ~2-2842 cm-1) before the
    200-2000 (or 400-3200) analysis window is applied. If the true global maximum of the
    raw experimental spectrum lies outside the analysis window (plausible, since C-H
    stretching bands ~2800-3000 cm-1 are often the most intense Raman feature in
    lipid-containing samples), the analysis window ends up normalized to only a narrow
    slice of [0,1], not the full 0-1 range -- which can badly distort RMSE/R^2 if not
    accounted for.

RECOMMENDATION: before citing or re-generating any numbers from run_final.py, either (a)
confirm RAMAN_MIN/RAMAN_MAX in the script match the 200-2000 cm-1 window actually used
for the cited results, and (b) confirm the experimental spectrum is windowed to that
range BEFORE min-max normalization is applied (not after). This does not invalidate the
existing official numbers in Section 4 -- output/processed/*.csv shows the experimental
curve for the correct window is properly normalized to [0, 1] -- but it means re-running
the current run_final.py as-is will not reproduce them.

10. FINAL CONCLUSION  [REVISED]
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This analysis fits the experimental LPS Raman spectrum as a non-negative linear mixture
of five independently DFT-simulated (B3LYP/6-31G(d)) fragment reference spectra --
established practice for spectral mixture analysis / classical least squares (Section 2),
though distinct from true fragment-based quantum chemistry methods (MTA/MIM/ONIOM) that
model the intact assembled molecule with inter-fragment coupling. The optimized model
gives a dominant, chemically sensible KDO contribution (47.3%) and a statistically
significant correlation with the experimental spectrum (Pearson r = 0.508), with two of
four independently-cited literature diagnostic peaks (carbohydrate/KDO ~989, lipid chain
~1131) reproduced within 10 cm-1 (Section 6). The modest R^2 (0.18) is shown, via a
regularized shape-flexibility sensitivity analysis (Section 7), to reflect missing
inter-fragment coupling and an incomplete fragment set rather than DFT lineshape
inaccuracy -- a specific, falsifiable, and testable claim rather than a general caveat.

Concrete next steps for improving the model (not required for this thesis submission,
but worth stating as future work):
  - Compute a slightly larger assembled fragment (e.g. KDO-lipid A or KDO2-lipid A core)
    to capture the glycosidic linkage and acyl-chain attachment directly, rather than
    five fully free monomers.
  - Add the missing outer-core sugars (glucose, galactose) to the fragment set.
  - If feasible, use ONIOM (available in Gaussian, already in use for this project) to
    treat the reactive core at DFT and peripheral chains at a cheaper level, approaching
    the intact-molecule fragment-quantum-chemistry approach described in Section 2a.
  - Acquire replicate experimental spectra (n>=3) to support statistical claims about
    fit quality.
