Main Reasons Reproducibility Is Difficult in SERS
1. Hotspots Are Random

SERS enhancement mainly comes from a few intense hotspots (nanogaps between particles).

If hotspot number, size, or location changes from chip to chip, signal changes.

EF∝∣E∣
4

Because enhancement scales strongly with field, tiny geometry changes create large signal variation.

2. Nanogap Variation

A 5 nm gap vs 10 nm gap can give very different enhancement.

During fabrication (sputtering, annealing, nanoparticle drying), gaps are not always identical.

3. Non-Uniform Surface Coverage

Different areas of the same chip may have:

more particles
fewer particles
clusters
smooth regions

So one Raman spot can differ from another spot.

4. Sample Distribution Is Uneven

When you drop-cast a sample droplet:

molecules may concentrate at edges (coffee-ring effect)
some hotspots get molecules, others do not

This causes spot-to-spot variation.

5. Laser Focusing Differences

Small changes in:

focus
spot position
objective height
integration time

can alter measured intensity.

6. Material Instability

Especially for silver:

oxidation
contamination
humidity effects

These change plasmonic behavior over time.

7. Complex Biological Samples

Real samples (blood, urine, proteins) vary in composition and matrix effects, making signals less consistent than pure dyes.

Why This Matters

Poor reproducibility makes it harder to use SERS for:

quantitative sensing
clinical diagnostics
comparing results across labs
regulatory approval

What Is Photoresist?

A photoresist is a light-sensitive polymer coating applied on a substrate (like silicon wafer).
When exposed to UV light through a mask, selected areas chemically change and can be removed (or remain) during development.

Simple meaning:

It is a temporary patterning layer used to draw microstructures on a wafer.

Two Main Types of Photoresist
1. Positive Photoresist

UV exposed area becomes soluble and is removed by developer.

Result:
Exposed parts disappear.

Good For:

Common lab use
2. Negative Photoresist

UV exposed area hardens and remains. Unexposed part is removed.

Result:
Exposed parts stay.

Good For:

Permanent molds
