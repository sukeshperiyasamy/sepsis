Biomarkers Investigated
N-Acetylmuramic Acid (NAM) – Peptidoglycan monomer (carbohydrate-rich; Gram-positive & Gram-negative relevance)

Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) – Gram-negative bacterial outer membrane marker (lipid + polysaccharide)

Lipoteichoic Acid (LTA) – Gram-positive bacterial cell wall marker (glycerophosphate polymer)

Phase 1: Sample & Stock Preparation
Prepared primary stock solutions for each biomarker:

1 mg biomarker dissolved in 1 mL solvent

Stock concentration: 1 mg/mL

Prepared serial 10× dilutions (10⁻¹ to 10⁻⁴ mg/mL) to cover:

High, medium, and low concentration ranges

Below-detection concentration regime for LOD analysis

Phase 2: Solvent & Buffer Strategy
Ultrapure water (Milli-Q, 18.2 MΩ·cm) selected as the primary solvent:

Minimal Raman background

No spectral overlap with biomarker fingerprint region

Optimal for peak identification and sensitivity studies

PBS intentionally avoided for SERS measurements due to:

Strong phosphate Raman bands (800–1100 cm⁻¹)

Overlap with NAM, LPS, and LTA characteristic peaks

Reduced molecule–substrate adsorption and signal reproducibility

Phase 3: SERS Spectral Acquisition
For each biomarker and concentration:

Identical drop-casting volume on SERS substrate

Same laser power, acquisition time, and optical settings

Multiple spectra collected per condition to assess:

Reproducibility

Signal stability

Sensor-to-sensor variability

Phase 4: Spectral Validation
Identified biomarker-specific Raman signatures:

NAM: Dominant carbohydrate and glycosidic vibrations (C–O, C–O–C)

LPS: Lipid-chain CH₂ modes and phosphate vibrations (lipid A region)

LTA: Phosphate backbone and C–O–P stretching modes

Confirmed:

Peak positions remain stable across concentrations

Signal intensity decreases systematically with dilution

Phase 5: Sensitivity & Limit of Detection (LOD)
Gradual dilution used to identify lowest detectable concentration.

LOD defined as:

“The lowest concentration at which characteristic biomarker peaks remain clearly distinguishable from noise.”

Representative outcome:

Peaks visible down to 10⁻³ mg/mL

Peaks not detectable at 10⁻⁴ mg/mL

Practical LOD ≈ 10⁻³ mg/mL

Phase 6: Data Consolidation
Compiled concentration vs. peak visibility data

Documented:

Signal-to-noise trends

Reproducibility across sensors

Strengths and limitations of the current SERS setup

Biochemical origins of the 785 nm excited SERS spectra of bacteria
(PMC4911336)

3. Major Experimental Observation (Critical Finding)
SERS spectra of whole bacterial cells and their surrounding supernatant are nearly identical.

Evidence:
Same peak positions

Same spectral shapes

Same molecular fingerprint

Interpretation:
Bacterial SERS signals do NOT originate from cell wall components (NAM, NAG, peptidoglycan).

Instead, signals arise from small, water-soluble metabolites secreted during washing and stress.

4. Identified Molecular Contributors
Through spectral fitting, bacterial SERS spectra were explained as a linear combination of purine metabolites:

Adenine (dominant; strong 739 cm⁻¹ band)

Hypoxanthine

Xanthine

Guanine

Uric acid

AMP

Key Insight:
No peptidoglycan, NAM, NAG, proteins, lipids, or nucleic acids are required to explain 785 nm bacterial SERS spectra.

5. Implication for NAM Detection
❌ Why NAM is NOT seen in most bacterial SERS:
NAM is buried within the thick peptidoglycan layer

SERS enhancement only effective within ~1–4 nm of metal surface

Cell wall thickness ≫ plasmonic enhancement distance

Purines dominate due to:

Higher Raman cross-section

Water solubility

Stress-induced secretion during washing

6. Effect of Sample Preparation
Buffer & Washing Effects (PMC5746774)
Borate buffer preserves SERS activity

Other buffers suppress SERS signal

Washing induces metabolic stress, increasing purine secretion

Conclusion: SERS acts as a metabolic stress sensor, not a structural probe.

10. Overall Conclusions (Take-Home Points)
✔ What SERS Detects Reliably:
Stress-induced extracellular metabolites

Purine degradation products

Metabolic state of bacteria

❌ What SERS Does NOT Detect Reliably:
NAM / NAG inside intact bacterial cell walls

Peptidoglycan structural features

🔬 Practical Implication for This Project:
NAM should be treated as a Raman biomarker (pure or extracted systems), not a native bacterial SERS biomarker.
