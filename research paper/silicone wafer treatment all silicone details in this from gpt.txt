Development and Validation of an AgNO₃-Coated Silicon SERS Substrate for Detection of N-Acetylmuramic Acid (NAM)
Abstract

Surface Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy (SERS) offers a powerful approach for rapid and label-free biomolecular detection. In this work, a low-cost AgNO₃-coated silicon SERS substrate was fabricated and evaluated using Rhodamine 6G (R6G) as a positive control and N-Acetylmuramic Acid (NAM, MurNAc) as the target bacterial biomarker. A complete data processing and validation pipeline was established, including spectral quality assessment, preprocessing, baseline correction, cosmic ray removal, peak validation, reproducibility analysis, and sensor optimization. While the fabricated substrate successfully detected R6G with high spectral accuracy, NAM detection remained weak and inconsistent, indicating that further substrate optimization is required before reliable bacterial biomarker detection can be achieved.

1. Introduction

Early detection of bacterial infections remains a major challenge in clinical diagnostics. N-Acetylmuramic Acid (NAM) is a structural component of bacterial peptidoglycan and represents a promising biomarker for bacterial identification and sepsis diagnostics.

Surface Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy (SERS) can significantly amplify Raman signals through localized surface plasmon resonance generated by metallic nanostructures. The objective of this work was to develop a low-cost silver-coated silicon SERS substrate and evaluate its ability to detect NAM.

To validate substrate performance, Rhodamine 6G (R6G), a standard Raman reporter molecule with strong Raman activity, was used as a positive control before evaluating NAM detection.

Abstract

Surface-Enhanced Raman Scattering (SERS) is a powerful analytical technique capable of detecting trace molecular species through electromagnetic enhancement generated by noble metal nanostructures. In this work, a nanostructured silicon-based SERS substrate was fabricated and evaluated for the detection of N-Acetylmuramic Acid (NAM), a key bacterial cell wall biomarker relevant to infection diagnostics.
