# Boronic Acid as the Capture Chemistry — Design Rationale

**Project:** SERS nanobiosensor for rapid bacterial identification in sepsis
**Prepared:** August 2026

---

## 1. Summary

Boronic acid is proposed as the bacterial capture element on the Ag/Si SERS
substrate. It forms reversible covalent bonds with cis-diols, which are abundant
on bacterial cell-wall glycans, and it can be immobilised on silver as a
self-assembled monolayer that doubles as a Raman reporter.

The approach is well precedented. Reported detection limits using
4-mercaptophenylboronic acid (4-MPBA) reach **8–13 CFU mL⁻¹** for respiratory
pathogens on a magnetic SERS strip [1], and capture efficiencies of **99.65% at
10³ CFU mL⁻¹** on 4-MPBA-functionalised silver dendrites [2].

Two limitations must be designed around: competing cis-diols in blood
(principally glucose) and the pH mismatch between simple phenylboronic acids and
physiological conditions. Both have established solutions.

---

## 2. The chemistry

Boronic acids condense with 1,2- or 1,3-cis-diols to form cyclic boronate esters,
releasing water. The reaction is reversible and pH-dependent: binding is stronger
above the pKa of the boronic acid, where the tetrahedral boronate anion
predominates over the neutral trigonal form [3].

```
R–B(OH)₂  +  HO–CH–CH–OH   ⇌   cyclic boronate ester  +  2 H₂O
```

This gives three properties that matter for a diagnostic device:

- **synthetic recognition** — no antibodies, no cold chain, no batch variability
- **reversibility** — the surface can be regenerated by lowering pH
- **broad glycan capture** — one ligand captures many bacterial species

---

## 3. Why 4-mercaptophenylboronic acid specifically

4-MPBA is bifunctional in exactly the way this device needs. The thiol
chemisorbs onto silver or gold, forming an ordered monolayer with the boronic
acid presented outward for capture [1].

More usefully, **4-MPBA is itself a strong Raman reporter**. Its SERS spectrum
shows characteristic bands at **1072 cm⁻¹** (C–S-stretch-coupled ring breathing)
and **1585 cm⁻¹** (aromatic C=C) [4]. Binding events perturb these bands, so the
same molecule performs capture and signal transduction.

This matters practically: it gives an internal reference. A change in the ratio
of reporter bands to analyte bands indicates binding, independent of absolute
intensity — which is the usual weakness of SERS quantification, since hotspot
density varies between substrates and between spots.

---

## 4. What boronic acid actually binds on bacteria — an important nuance

The literature commonly states that boronic acid captures bacteria by binding
**peptidoglycan** [1]. More careful studies attribute the interaction primarily
to **lipopolysaccharide in Gram-negative organisms and teichoic acids in
Gram-positive organisms** [5, 6].

**The structural argument favours the second interpretation, and it is directly
relevant to our NAM work.**

Boronate ester formation requires two hydroxyls in a suitable cis geometry. In
the peptidoglycan glycan backbone:

| Position | N-acetylmuramic acid | N-acetylglucosamine |
|---|---|---|
| C-1 | glycosidic linkage (in polymer) | glycosidic linkage |
| C-2 | acetamido — **not a hydroxyl** | acetamido — **not a hydroxyl** |
| C-3 | D-lactyl ether — **blocked** | free OH |
| C-4 | glycosidic linkage | free OH |

In NAM, C-2 carries the acetamido group and C-3 the lactyl ether, so no
1,2-diol is available. In GlcNAc, the C-3 and C-4 hydroxyls exist but are
**trans-diequatorial**, a geometry that binds boronic acid weakly. In the intact
polymer, C-1 and C-4 are further consumed by the β-1,4 glycosidic linkages.

**Conclusion:** the peptidoglycan glycan backbone is a poor boronic acid target.
Capture on intact bacteria is far more likely occurring through LPS — whose
O-antigen sugars and Kdo/heptose inner core are rich in cis-diols — and through
wall and lipoteichoic acids on Gram-positives. Recent work reports a 40-fold
binding enhancement from bivalent boron conjugates acting mainly on wall teichoic
acid [5].

### Why this is worth stating explicitly

It means the capture chemistry and the NAM detection chemistry target
**different parts of the cell**. That is a deliberate design choice, not an
oversight, and framing it that way is better than letting a reviewer notice it.

It also makes a **testable prediction**: capture efficiency should differ
systematically between Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms, because the
diol density and accessibility differ. Boronic acid-modified dendrimers have
already been used to discriminate Gram types on exactly this basis [6] — which
both supports the approach and means our contribution must be differentiated
from it.

---

## 5. Limitation 1 — competing cis-diols in blood

Boronic acid binds any cis-diol. Blood contains glucose at roughly 5 mM, plus
glycoproteins, ribose-containing nucleotides and catechols.

Reported phenylboronic acid affinities follow the order
**fructose > arabinose, ribose > galactose > glucose**, spanning only one to two
orders of magnitude [7]. That selectivity is inadequate when the interferent
exceeds the target by many orders of magnitude in concentration.

**Mitigations, in order of practicality:**

1. **Sample preparation.** Separate or lyse bacteria and wash before capture.
   This is the most reliable route and is compatible with the microfluidic
   front-end already planned.
2. **Boronic acids with poor glucose affinity.** Glucose binds relatively weakly
   among common sugars; ligand choice can widen the margin.
3. **Multivalent presentation.** A bacterium presents many diols simultaneously,
   so a multivalent surface gains avidity that a monovalent sugar like glucose
   cannot match. This is the strongest conceptual argument for why the approach
   works at all in biological media.
4. **Ratiometric readout.** Using the 4-MPBA reporter bands as an internal
   reference reduces sensitivity to non-specific binding.

---

## 6. Limitation 2 — pH

Simple phenylboronic acids have pKa near **8.8** [8]. Blood is pH 7.4, below the
pKa, where the weakly binding neutral trigonal form dominates. Binding is
therefore poor at exactly the pH the device must operate at.

**Solutions:**

**Benzoboroxoles.** Ring strain from the fused five-membered ring lowers the pKa
to approximately **7.2–7.3** [8, 9]. They bind efficiently at physiological pH
and show higher saccharide affinity than other Wulff-type boronic acids. They
also bind non-reducing sugars — methyl α-D-mannopyranoside, methyl
β-D-galactopyranoside — that simple boronic acids cannot [8].

**Fluorinated boronic acids.** Electron-withdrawing substituents such as ortho-
fluorine lower the pKa. 3-fluoro-4-mercaptophenylboronic acid retains the thiol
anchor while shifting the working pH downward.

**Wulff-type boronic acids.** An ortho-aminomethyl group provides intramolecular
B–N interaction, stabilising the tetrahedral form at lower pH.

**Recommendation:** evaluate a benzoboroxole derivative alongside 4-MPBA.
4-MPBA is the established SERS reporter and the sensible starting point; a
benzoboroxole variant is the likely route to working in undiluted physiological
fluid.

---

## 7. How this fits the device architecture

The design separates **capture** from **identification**:

| Stage | Element | Question answered |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | Boronic acid monolayer on Ag/Si | Is bacterial material present, and concentrate it at the hotspots |
| Identification | SERS fingerprint + DFT-assigned bands | Gram-positive or Gram-negative? |

This is deliberate. Requiring a single element to be both broadly capturing and
highly discriminating is the usual failure mode of affinity biosensors. Here the
boronic acid needs only to be broad and robust, while discrimination comes from
the vibrational fingerprint — which is where the NAM reference spectrum and the
band assignments already completed become directly useful.

Concentrating analyte at the plasmonic surface also addresses the central
practical problem in SERS: enhancement falls off steeply with distance from the
metal, so a capture layer that holds the target within a few nanometres is worth
orders of magnitude in signal.

---

## 8. Proposed experimental sequence

1. **Establish reproducible Ag/Si substrates** — prerequisite; currently blocked
   by the contamination issue under investigation.
2. **Form and verify the 4-MPBA monolayer.** Confirm the 1072 and 1585 cm⁻¹
   bands are present, stable, and reproducible across substrates. This doubles as
   a substrate quality-control metric, which the current fabrication work needs
   anyway.
3. **Model diol binding.** Test with a simple sugar to confirm the reporter bands
   respond to boronate ester formation.
4. **Bacterial capture in buffer.** Establish capture efficiency and detection
   limit for one Gram-positive and one Gram-negative organism.
5. **Compare Gram types.** Test the prediction that capture efficiency differs.
6. **Move to serum, then blood.** Quantify the glucose interference and evaluate
   whether sample preparation or a lower-pKa ligand is required.
7. **Regeneration.** Establish how many bind–release cycles the surface tolerates.

Steps 2 and 3 can begin as soon as substrate reproducibility is restored, and
would give an independent handle on substrate quality.

---

## 9. Open questions

- Does 4-MPBA monolayer formation interfere with the Ag/Si hotspot structure, or
  passivate the surface?
- What is the actual capture efficiency in whole blood versus buffer?
- Does bound bacterial material sit close enough to the surface for its own
  Raman bands to be enhanced, or do we only observe the reporter?
- Is boronic acid alone sufficient for the sensitivity required in sepsis, where
  bacterial loads can be 1–10 CFU mL⁻¹?
- How does the boronic acid layer affect the photostability limits established
  for NAM powder? Plasmonic substrates concentrate the field and may lower the
  damage threshold considerably.
