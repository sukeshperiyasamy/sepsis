Clinical Urgency of Sepsis
• Approximately 49 million sepsis cases globally (2020) with ~11 million deaths.
• Early detection and treatment are critical; delays increase mortality.
• Sepsis is a major contributor to hospital fatalities and ICU burden.

Limitations of Current Diagnostics
● Blood culture: slow (24–72 hours) and insensitive when antibiotics were
given.
● Host markers (CRP, PCT) are non-specific and can be elevated in
non-bacterial states .
● Clinical scores (qSOFA/SIRS): supportive, not definitive.
● Molecular assays (PCR) require lab infrastructure and known targets.
● Empirical broad-spectrum antibiotic use is common, driving antimicrobial
resistance.

Why Bacterial-Specific Biomarkers?
● Early diagnosis and improved clinical outcomes .
● Directly indicate presence of bacteria (not just host inflammation).
● Enable Gram classification.Reduce unnecessary broad-spectrum antibiotic
use and combat AMR.

SERS Nanobiosensor: Overview
• SERS = Surface-Enhanced Raman Spectroscopy; provides molecular
fingerprints.
• Plasmonic nanostructures (Ag/Au) create strong local fields (hotspots).
• Combine SERS substrate with capture probes (aptamers/antibodies) for
selectivity.

SERS Fundamentals
• Raman scattering measures vibrational modes; specific peaks = molecule
signature.
• Electromagnetic enhancement from plasmon resonance scales..
• Hotspots (nanogaps) give the largest enhancement; single-molecule sensitivity
possible.

Nanomaterials & Substrate Design
• Choice of metal (Ag stronger SERS; Au more stable) affects resonance.
• Particle geometry (stars, rods, gaps) tunes hotspot intensity.
• Substrate uniformity & reproducible fabrication are essential for clinical use.

Excitation Wavelength & Practical Considerations
• Common lasers: 532 nm (resonant dyes), 633 nm, 785 nm (bio-friendly), 1064
nm (high autofluorescence samples).
• Balance: stronger scattering (shorter λ) vs. fluorescence/background and sample
heating.
• Laser power and exposure time must be optimized for clinical samples.

Workflow
Pipeline:

Diagnostic Workflow (Patient → Answer)
• 1) Sample collection at bedside/lab.
• 2) Sample prep (optional: concentrate, lyse or microfluidic separation).
• 3) Apply to SERS biosensor (capture probes bind LPS/LTA/PGN).
• 4) Acquire Raman spectrum and analyze for biomarker peaks.
• 5) Output: presence/absence of bacterial markers and Gram classification.

Clinical Utility & Impact
• Rapid Gram-level info enables earlier targeted antibiotics (improves survival).
• Less empiric broad-spectrum use → supports antimicrobial stewardship.
• Portable SERS devices can enable near-patient, point-of-care testing in ED/ICU.

Challenges & Roadmap
● Extensive literature review done and finalization of biomarker .
● SERS simulations initiated to optimize spectral response and substrate
design.
● Next: experimental validation of simulation results using standard samples.
● Prototype fabrication and spectral benchmarking planned in next phase.
● Key challenges: ensuring signal reproducibility, substrate uniformity, and
scaling for real-world use.
● Long-term goal: translate optimized SERS workflow into a portable sepsis
diagnostic device.
