LPS: Structure, Significance & Modeling Strategy
What is LPS?
Major outer membrane component of Gram-negative bacteria
Critical biomarker for bacterial sepsis detection
Three structural components:
Lipid A (toxic core) Core oligosaccharide O-antigen
Why fragment-based DFT modeling?
Full LPS = tens of thousands of atoms → computationally infeasible for full QM modeling
Representative fragment Kdo (3-deoxy-D-manno-octulosonic acid) used as core sugar model
Validated by DFT using B3LYP/6-31G(d,p) in Gaussian 03
Solvation effects included via PCM + explicit water molecules
Key functional groups (dominate spectra)
–COO⁻ carboxyl –PO₄⁻ phosphate –OH hydroxyl CH₂/CH₃ lipid chains
These functional groups are responsible for metal binding, structural stability, and vibrational signatures — the basis of the Raman fingerprint.
Computational validation
DFT results agree with
NMR + spectroscopic experiments
Same DFT framework that produces IR spectra also yields Raman activity — directly applicable to this work.

LPS vs PGN vs LTA: Differentiation by Raman
LPS (Gram-negative)
Lipid A + Core + O-antigen
Many strong peaks
Complex, feature-rich spectrum
Both lipid AND carbohydrate signals
Peaks at 857, 1003, 1333, 1614 cm⁻¹
PGN (Gram-positive)
Peptidoglycan layer
Fewer peaks
Simpler spectral profile
Mainly carbohydrate/protein signals
Partial overlap at ~1333 cm⁻¹
LTA (Gram-positive)
Lipoteichoic acid
Fewer peaks
Simpler spectral profile
Mainly carbohydrate/protein signals
Partial overlap at ~1614 cm⁻¹
Overlapping peaks (challenge)
~1333 cm⁻¹ → LPS + PGN + LTA ~1614 cm⁻¹ → present in all three
Overlapping peaks mean single-peak identification is unreliable. Differentiation requires multi-peak pattern analysis or chemometrics (PCA, ML).
SERS sample preparation (Yang et al., 2022)
LPS extracted via hot phenol–water method or purchased (Sigma-Aldrich)
Concentration: 100 µg/mL in deionized water
2 µL deposited on silver nanorod (AgNR) SERS substrate, air-dried at ~20°C
Detection at ~8.75 picogram level
Laser: 785 nm excitation
Laser power: ~9 mW, acquisition: 10 seconds
Multiple spectra collected per sample for reproducibility
Pre-processing: baseline correction → noise removal → normalization

Frequency-shift SERS concept
Detection based on peak shifts (not just presence) — e.g., ~2933 → ~2922 cm⁻¹ upon LPS binding to antibody. More sensitive than intensity-only methods.
SERS vs conventional methods
No labels or dyes required
Faster and simpler than ELISA / LAL test
Anti-lipid A antibody used → targets most conserved toxic region
